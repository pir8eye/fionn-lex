The Fionn Lex Protocol

The Inverse Aristotle Chair & Pedagogical Cryptography Architecture

Project Overview

The Fionn Lex Protocol is a tabletop educational architecture designed to teach advanced cryptographic concepts through direct physical manipulation, spatial reasoning, and asymmetric collaboration—bypassing mathematical abstraction for non-abstract learners.

Core Mechanics

  • 44-Glyph Matrix: 26 Latin letters + 18 phonetic digraphs generated systematically using circles and line iterations.
  • Tactile Rotations: Modulo arithmetic translates into physical key rotations and visual grid overlays.
  • Google-Proof: Original geometric bytecode cannot be solved via online AI or search engines.

Primary Artifacts

  • The Field Guide: Deterministic SVG-generated orthography.
  • The 1755 Johnson Dictionary: Primary source text used as dynamic, non-linear shift keys.
  • Asymmetric Sealed Envelopes: Peer-to-peer key splits requiring mutual player trust.

The Pedagogy of Trust & Assumed Competence

"Educational systems often operate on a deficit model: 'The student is empty; we must pour abstract knowledge into them.' Your approach operates on a trust model: 'The student is already a natural problem solver. We trust them enough to hand them a complex physical puzzle.'"

Key Philosophical Pillars

  1. The Inverse Aristotle Chair: Derive abstract "Forms" (cryptographic theory) by physically interacting with tangible "Matter" (grids, artifacts, books).
  2. Assumed Competence: Respecting the player's intelligence by eliminating trivializing shortcuts and hand-holding.
  3. Asymmetric Social Engine: Trust is both the pedagogical foundation and the cryptographic requirement (Red/Blue key blending).
